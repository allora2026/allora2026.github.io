The handover card is the starting point

Published September 23, 2026

A handover is not the document someone sends at the end. It is the starting point the next team actually receives. If they still need a meeting to discover what happened, why it happened, and what not to do again, the document was merely a souvenir.

This is the compact card I would use after a deployment decision, an incident fix, or an operational change. Five fields. No heroic transcript burial. Enough shape for the next person—or an approved tool—to begin with judgment instead of archaeology.

The five fields

  1. Source. The runbook, decision record, issue, file, or public reference that makes the claim checkable.
  2. Decision. What was chosen, including the constraint that made the choice reasonable.
  3. Operating context. The service, team, project, or boundary that tells the next actor where this applies—and where it does not.
  4. Next action. The concrete move that should happen next: verify, deploy, monitor, ask, update, or stop.
  5. Correction or lesson. The exception, changed assumption, or sharper rule that the next comparable task should inherit.

A neutral example

Imagine a team adjusts a deployment check after discovering that the old check was too broad. The card is not a customer story; it is deliberately ordinary: 

source: deployment runbook, section “pre-release checks”
decision: narrow the check to the service that owns the change
operating context: production release workflow; not a substitute for incident review
next action: run the revised check on the next release and record the result
correction / lesson: a green aggregate signal did not prove this service was ready

Notice what this refuses to do. It does not pretend the final sentence is the whole truth. It keeps a route to the source, states the scope, leaves a next move, and makes the correction portable. That is enough to turn a one-off answer into something the next task can challenge and use.

Put the card where the work can retrieve it

A card in a private note app is better than amnesia, but it still creates a scavenger hunt. The useful version lives with the team’s controlled context: near the runbooks, decisions, files, and prior fixes that can make it legible. The point is not to archive more prose. The point is to make the next entry into work less blind.

That is a natural fit for Usable: a governed home for fragments, files, collections, workspaces, roles, and approved AI access; source-returning search; and solution writeback when the work earns a better rule. Technical teams can meet the same context through browser chat, MCP-compatible tools, or an API-backed workflow. Different doors, one accountable record.

Try the card once on a recurring handover. Then, on the next comparable task, retrieve it before starting and ask the only question that matters: did it make the work more specific, or merely more documented? If it is just documentation, correct the card. If it is a real starting point, give it a governed home.
