Three things to retrieve before a client delivery task.

Published November 25, 2026

The test for delivery continuity is not whether the team can find a folder. It is whether the person about to do the work can retrieve the few things that make starting safe and specific.

Run this on a real discovery, implementation, or support task—not a tidy retrospective. Put the task owner in front of the material they are allowed to use. Do not accept a verbal tour. Ask them to retrieve these three things.

1. The client or project context that applies

They should be able to name the engagement, the current outcome, and the context that belongs to it. “It is somewhere in the project folder” is not an answer. Nor is a generic practice template that happens to contain familiar words.

The point is scope. Before a task moves, the owner needs to distinguish this client’s material from adjacent work and from reusable delivery knowledge.

2. The source or decision that governs the work

Next, retrieve the standard, decision, meeting outcome, implementation note, or other source that makes the next move inspectable. The result should not be a confident paraphrase with no path back to evidence. It should be a source someone can open and question.

This is deliberately modest. It does not promise that a system will choose for the team or replace delivery judgment. It asks whether the people doing the work can see what the work rests on.

3. The workspace boundary the task may use

Finally, establish which workspace is permitted for this task—and which people or approved assistants may retrieve from it. A useful-looking answer drawn from the wrong client context is not a productivity gain. It is a boundary failure wearing good prose.

Write down the three results. If the owner cannot produce them without reconstructing the story from chat, folders, and senior colleagues, the handover is incomplete. The problem is not a lack of stored files. It is a missing retrieval path.

What to do with the result

Do not turn this into a ceremony. Use it to expose one concrete repair: clarify the applicable project context, attach the governing source to the work, or correct the workspace boundary. Then repeat the check on the next similar task.
