A guidance page is not a service workflow.

Published October 20, 2026

A rule can be online and still be absent when a case begins. The person handling it may find the page but not know whether this is the current source, which service scope it governs, or where a useful clarification belongs after the work is done.

That is not a complaint about documentation. Guidance pages matter. The category error is treating their existence as proof that a service workflow can use them. A service workflow needs a visible route from the question in front of it to the source that applies, the boundary that limits it, and the reviewed lesson that should survive the case.

Use a service-guidance receipt

Before treating a page as operational guidance, make a tiny receipt. Four fields are enough to expose most of the useful friction:

  1. The question or case. Name the request, decision, eligibility check, or service situation being handled. “Find the policy” is not a task; it is a postponement wearing a lanyard.
  2. The authoritative source and revision. Point to the rule, procedure, decision, or guidance that governs this case, including the revision a responsible person should inspect.
  3. The applicable boundary. State the service, team, workspace, or access scope that makes the source relevant. A source may be authoritative without being universal.
  4. The reviewed clarification. When the case exposes a missing condition or a clearer explanation, record where that proposed lesson goes for review so the next case does not rebuild it from scraps.

This is not a policy engine

The receipt does not interpret rules automatically. It does not decide a case, certify compliance, or make ambiguity disappear. It makes ambiguity inspectable before someone quietly substitutes confidence for evidence.

That distinction is worth defending. Consistency is not copied wording. Two staff members can repeat the same sentence and still act differently if they cannot see which source applies, what boundary governs it, or how the last exception was resolved. The fix is not a grander document library. It is a better path into the work.

Give the next case a governed home

Usable describes a controlled place for public-service rules, procedures, prior decisions, and internal guidance, with workspaces and access boundaries so staff and approved AI tools can work from the same source. Its public product material also describes retrieval that brings the source back and solution writeback for lessons worth reusing.
