A template is not a permission slip

Published October 1, 2026

A useful template tells the next team how to work. It does not entitle them to the last client’s context.

That sounds obvious until the closeout folder becomes the template library. Then the checklist arrives with a decision trail, the decision trail arrives with names and constraints, and “reuse” quietly turns into copying more than the next engagement needs.

The boundary test

Before promoting anything from an engagement, ask three questions:

  1. Would this still teach a useful method if the client facts disappeared? If yes, it may be a delivery standard.
  2. Does the next team need this specific decision, source, or constraint to do its own work? If no, it is not template material.
  3. Who should be able to retrieve it next? The answer determines whether it belongs with a shared standard, a customer workspace, or an unresolved review.

The point is not to sterilise the work until nothing remains. A pattern without its conditions is cargo-cult theatre. Keep enough explanation for the standard to be tested, then keep the client record where that record belongs.

Make the template carry the right thing

A clean delivery template can hold a sequence, a review question, an implementation lesson, and the sources a future team should consult. It should not smuggle in another client’s working context merely because that context happened to sit beside the useful lesson.

This is why folders alone are a weak model. They can store a document, but they do not answer the more important question: which people, assistants, customers, or workflows should be permitted to use this material now?

Reuse needs a home, not a shrug

Usable describes reusable client knowledge as client context, delivery standards, meeting outcomes, and implementation lessons—held in controlled workspaces. Its public material also describes setting workspace access so the right team, assistant, customer, or product surface can use approved memory through the appropriate interface.

Run the boundary test on one template from your last engagement. What should compound into the next piece of work—and what must stay behind the boundary? If the answer needs a governed home rather than another shared folder, explore Usable’s use cases.
