Practical note · pre-task retrieval

The next client task needs context, not a folder tour.

Published November 24, 2026

A client folder can preserve material. It cannot tell the next responsible person what matters now. Before an implementation, advisory, discovery, or support task begins, someone still has to establish the applicable client context, the delivery source or decision that governs the work, and the boundary they are permitted to use.

That is where continuity often breaks. A team receives a tidy handover and still spends the first hour asking which notes are current, whether a standard applies to this client, and what an approved assistant may actually retrieve. The folder tour has preserved documents. It has not produced a safe starting point.

Run the pre-task check

Choose one recurring delivery task. Before anyone starts, ask three questions:

  1. What client or project context applies? Identify the scope, current outcome, and material that belongs to this engagement—not a similarly named project or a general delivery archive.
  2. Which source or decision governs the work? Retrieve the relevant standard, implementation note, meeting outcome, or decision record so the task begins from something inspectable.
  3. Which workspace boundary may this person or approved tool use? Access is part of the answer. A useful source in the wrong client context is not a shortcut; it is a mistake waiting for an attractive sentence.

This is not a demand for perfect documentation or a claim that a system can understand a client for you. Delivery judgment remains human work. The test is smaller and sharper: can the owner begin from the context, source, and boundary that actually apply, instead of rebuilding them from folders and memory?

Make the starting point retrievable

Consultancies need both continuity and separation. A delivery standard may be useful across work; client-specific context should remain scoped to the engagement that gives it meaning. When those two things are mixed into a folder tree, the next task gets either too little context or too much of the wrong kind.

Usable is built for this shape of work: teams can keep client context, delivery standards, meeting outcomes, and implementation lessons in owned workspaces; access boundaries keep knowledge scoped; source-returning retrieval brings the material back before a person or approved AI tool starts to write or help. A reviewed solution can then become a lesson for the next task without turning every client record into general inventory.

Try the check on one task this week. Can its owner retrieve the applicable context, the governing source, and the permitted boundary before work begins? If not, the team has stored material. It does not yet have usable delivery context. Explore Usable’s consultancy and client-handover use cases.
