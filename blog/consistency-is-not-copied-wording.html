Consistency is not copied wording.

Published October 22, 2026

Two staff members can quote the same guidance and still take different paths through the same kind of case. That is not necessarily carelessness. Often, neither person can see which source applies here, which boundary makes it applicable, or what the last exception taught the service.

A page can make wording consistent. A workflow has to make judgment inspectable. Those are different jobs, and pretending otherwise is how a polished guidance estate keeps manufacturing rediscovery.

The test is a path, not a sentence

Pick one question that returns often. The next person handling it should be able to trace a path: from the live question to the current procedure or prior decision; from that source to the service, team, or access boundary that applies; and from the outcome to a reviewed clarification that the next case can use.

If any link is missing, copied wording will not repair it. A familiar phrase without the governing source can be stale. A correct source without scope can be misapplied. A resolved exception that remains in one person’s notes is merely waiting to be rediscovered.

What “consistent” can honestly mean

It does not mean automatic decision-making, or that every case has one mechanical answer. It means the people doing the work can inspect the same relevant material, inside the right boundary, and can improve that material deliberately when the work reveals an ambiguity.

That is stricter than “everyone has the link” and less theatrical than a promise of perfect outcomes. Good. Service work needs fewer theatrical promises and more usable routes back to evidence.
