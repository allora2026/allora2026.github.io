A client handover is a memory boundary, not a document dump.

Published October 27, 2026

A handover can contain every file and still fail. The next delivery team opens a folder, finds a meeting trail, and then has to ask the same person to explain what is current, what applies to this engagement, and what they are actually allowed to use.

That is not a documentation shortage. It is a missing boundary around memory. A useful handover does not merely transfer material; it gives the next team a way to retrieve the relevant context without turning client history into ambient knowledge.

The handover receipt has four fields

  1. Scope: What client, workstream, or delivery moment is this for?
  2. Sources and decisions: Which records show the current decision, delivery standard, or implementation detail?
  3. Boundary: Which team or workspace may retrieve and use this material?
  4. Reviewed lesson: What did this work teach that should be captured for the next engagement or the next handover?

None of those questions requires a grand process. They are a quick test for whether the next team has an inspectable route into the work, rather than a larger archive to search and a longer explanation to request.

Why the boundary belongs in the receipt

Consultancies need two kinds of continuity at once: client-specific context that stays with the engagement, and reusable delivery knowledge that improves the next one. Those are adjacent, not interchangeable. A project decision can be essential to the next assigned team while being irrelevant—or inappropriate—outside that project. A delivery standard can become more useful when it is deliberately separated from the client history that produced it.

The receipt makes that distinction visible before somebody copies a useful-looking note into the wrong shared space. Better than discovering the boundary only after the knowledge has already travelled.

Give the next team a governed starting point

Usable describes workspaces for organising procedures, decisions, files, and reusable context by customer or project; its public features also describe access boundaries, source-returning search, and solution writeback. That is the shape a handover needs: relevant material available to the people and approved AI tools working inside the right scope, plus a place for the reviewed implementation lesson to survive the project.

Try the receipt on one live handover that still depends on a person translating the project’s history. Can the next team retrieve the current sources inside the right boundary—and capture the reviewed lesson for the next engagement?
