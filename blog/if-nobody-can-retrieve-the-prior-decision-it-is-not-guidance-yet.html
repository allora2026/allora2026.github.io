If nobody can retrieve the prior decision, it is not guidance yet.

Published November 19, 2026

A decision that only lives in the head of the person who made it is not guidance. It is a temporary advantage with a calendar attached. The moment the case returns—or the responsible person changes—the organisation is back to inference, confidence, and luck.

This is the quiet failure beneath many apparently consistent service answers. The wording survives. The reason does not. A staff member, or an approved AI tool, can repeat a familiar response without being able to show the prior decision that shaped it, the source material behind it, or the boundary that governs its reuse.

The retrieval test

Take one recurring question that has an exception, a past ruling, or a rule that changed. Ask the next responsible person three things:

  1. Can you retrieve the prior decision? Not a retelling of it—a record, source, or approved piece of guidance that can be inspected.
  2. Can you see what it relied on? A prior decision is useful only when its supporting rule, procedure, or source is still close enough to challenge or confirm.
  3. Can you tell where it applies? A decision made for one service, role, customer context, or workspace should not drift into another merely because it is easy to find.

Fail any one of those tests and the team may still have a useful answer. It does not yet have durable guidance. The distinction matters because a polished answer can travel much farther than the evidence that justified it.

Make judgment carry forward without making it universal

The point is not to turn every judgment into an automatic decision, or to strip people of discretion. It is to give the next person a legible starting point: the current source, the prior reasoning where it is relevant, and the limits of the context they are permitted to use. Judgment remains human work. Reconstruction should not be the default operating model.

That is a practical shape for governed organisational memory. Usable lets teams keep procedures, decisions, files, and reusable context in owned workspaces; it supports access boundaries and retrieval that brings the source back. People and approved AI tools can then work from the material they are permitted to use, rather than treating a stray answer as universal policy.

Choose one recurring answer that would be hardest to explain from its source next month. If the prior decision cannot be retrieved, inspected, and scoped, it is not guidance yet. See how Usable approaches governed organisational memory.
