The second use is the test.

Published October 7, 2026

The first time an onboarding answer helps, it can look finished. Someone asks where a procedure lives, receives a tidy response, and gets back to work. That is a useful moment. It is not yet a learning system.

The test arrives on the next comparable task: can another person retrieve the supporting source, stay inside the answer’s intended scope, and put a correction where the team will actually see it? If not, the organisation has saved a sentence. It has not saved the work.

Keep a review receipt

The answer card records the question, source, boundary, and update path. This receipt records what happened when someone actually used it. Keep it short:

  • Source reached: could the reader get to the actual procedure, decision, file, or record—not merely a link that exists?
  • Scope understood: could they tell which team, customer context, or workflow the answer applied to—and what it did not authorize?
  • Next action changed: did it help them complete the work without returning to a senior person for the missing context?
  • Lesson captured: if reality had moved, did the team put the improvement where its own review process can consider it?

Why the receipt matters

Teams often mistake a well-written FAQ for durable knowledge because the first interaction is frictionless. The second use exposes the hidden dependencies: a source only one person can find, a rule that silently changed, a customer detail that was copied too broadly, or a correction that dies in a chat reply.

This is not a demand for a documentation ceremony. Pick one repeated onboarding answer. Let a newcomer use it. Record what held and what broke. Then repair the path, not just the prose. Boring in the right places is a feature: the next reader should not have to guess whether an answer is evidence, opinion, or an expired shortcut.

A home for the second use

Usable provides the pieces that make this review practical: workspaces for owned knowledge, access boundaries for the people and tools allowed to use it, reusable source material in fragments, files, and collections, and solution writeback for lessons from completed work. It is a governed place to keep the answer, its sources, and what its use taught the team.
